# Code of Conduct

AVP is an open technical project. Participation is expected to support rigorous, respectful, evidence-oriented collaboration across protocol design, implementation, security, and conformance work.

## Expected behavior

Participants must:

- discuss ideas, implementations, and evidence rather than attack people;
- disagree constructively and explain technical trade-offs;
- respect different backgrounds, identities, experience levels, and communication styles;
- avoid exposing credentials, private data, embargoed vulnerabilities, or other sensitive material;
- accept maintainer moderation intended to keep project spaces safe and technically productive;
- disclose relevant conflicts of interest when they could materially affect standards or governance decisions.

## Unacceptable behavior

The project does not tolerate harassment, threats, discrimination, sexualized conduct, deliberate intimidation, stalking, doxxing, sustained personal attacks, bad-faith disruption, impersonation, or publication of another person's private information without permission.

Technical disagreement is not harassment by itself. Criticism may be direct when it remains focused on observable behavior, evidence, specifications, code, or process rather than a person's worth or identity.

## Scope

This Code applies in repository issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, security coordination, project meetings, and other spaces where a participant is representing AVP or acting on behalf of the project.

## Reporting

For ordinary moderation concerns, contact the current maintainer through a private contact method listed on their GitHub profile. Security vulnerabilities must follow `SECURITY.md` instead of this process.

Reports should identify the relevant project space, describe the behavior, include available evidence, and avoid unnecessary redistribution of sensitive material. Maintainers will limit report details to people who need them for review and response.

## Enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for interpreting and enforcing this Code consistently and proportionately. Depending on severity, pattern, context, and prior behavior, responses may include:

1. a private or public clarification or warning;
2. removal or editing of disruptive content where platform controls permit it;
3. temporary restrictions on participation;
4. removal from project roles or project spaces;
5. permanent exclusion for severe or repeated violations.

Immediate protective action may be taken when conduct creates a credible safety, privacy, or security risk. Enforcement decisions should document enough rationale for accountability without unnecessarily exposing reporters or affected participants.

Maintainers must recuse themselves from deciding a report when they are the subject of the report or have a material conflict of interest. When no unconflicted maintainer is available, the project should seek an appropriate independent reviewer before taking a non-urgent final action.

## No retaliation

Retaliation against a good-faith reporter, witness, or participant in a moderation process is itself a violation of this Code.

## Project evolution

As the maintainer group grows, AVP may adopt a widely used community code and a dedicated reporting channel. Any replacement must preserve clear reporting, confidentiality, conflict-of-interest handling, and proportionate enforcement.
